# Race Preparation Knowledge

## Nutrition Plan

### Race Week (assumes Sunday race day)

**Monday–Wednesday:** Slightly increase protein intake, decrease carbs.

**Thursday–Friday:** Reverse — increase carb intake substantially (carb loading phase). For shorter races (5K/10K), carb loading is less critical but maintaining good nutrition still matters.

**Hydration (if warm forecast):** Start drinking extra water on Thursday and continue through Friday. Include 1–2 electrolyte drinks per day (e.g. sugar-free Gatorade) — the salt helps retain water. If race is on a different day, shift the schedule so carb loading begins 2 days before and hydration begins 2 days before.

### Race Morning

- Eat breakfast 2 hours before the start.
- Focus on fructose + glucose: 1 banana + 1 bagel with cream cheese.
- Drink water or Gatorade with breakfast.
- Fueling gels before the start:
  - 5K/10K: optional - not needed.
  - Half marathon: 1 gel 5 minutes before start.
  - Marathon+: 1 gel 5 minutes before start.

### During the Race

- **5K/10K:** No fueling needed. Water at stations if available.
- **Half marathon:** 1 gel every 30–40 minutes. Water at every station.
- **Marathon:** 1 gel every 30–40 minutes. Water at every available station.
- **Ultra:** Follow a structured nutrition plan with gels, real food, and electrolytes at regular intervals.
- General rule: do not wait until hungry or thirsty — by that point it is too late. Eat and drink on schedule.

## Race Day Preparation

### Golden Rule

Nothing new on race day. This includes food, gear, shoes, and warm-up routine. Only use what has been tried and tested during training.

### Night Before

- Set out full race outfit, gels, bib, and all race morning essentials Friday night. This reduces race morning stress significantly.
- Plan logistics: how to get to the start line and how to get home after the race.

### Dressing

- Dress for the forecasted conditions during the race, not the morning.
- If the morning is cold but the race will be warm, wear disposable layers over running clothes that can be discarded before the start.

### Warm-Up

- 10-minute gentle jog before the race start.
- Finish with 4 strides/accelerations.
- If part of the athlete's routine, perform dynamic stretches before the race.
- If cold while waiting, do pogo jumps in place to stay warm.
- For shorter races (5K/10K), a thorough warm-up is more important since the race starts at a higher intensity.

## Race Pace Strategy

### Short Races (5K/10K)

- **First mile:** Start controlled. Resist the adrenaline surge — settle into rhythm.
- **Middle miles:** Lock into target pace. This should feel hard but sustainable.
- **Final mile/km:** If feeling strong, gradually increase effort. Leave everything on the course.
- Pacing is more aggressive than longer races — running near threshold the entire time. A slight positive split (first half faster) is normal for 5K. Aim for even splits in 10K.

### Half Marathon

- **Miles 1–3:** Start at or slightly slower than goal pace. Do not go out fast.
- **Miles 3–10:** Settle into goal pace. Stay consistent and fuel at mile 7–8.
- **Miles 10–13.1:** This is the race. If energy was conserved early, pick up the pace in the final 5K. Use a mantra for mental focus.

### Marathon

- **Miles 1–3:** Deliberately slow start, as if still warming up. Do not weave through crowds — let others pass. They will be caught later.
- **Miles 3–10:** Gradually build pace but stay slightly below race pace. Conserve energy and keep the aerobic engine running. Starting too fast triggers anaerobic energy usage and lactic acid buildup.
- **Miles 10–20:** Lock into target race pace (the pace trained at during tempo runs). Hydrate and fuel at every station.
- **Miles 20–26.2:** The real race begins. The athlete has trained for this — long runs have prepared both mind and body. Maintain pace and gradually increase speed if possible. Use a personal mantra. If energy was conserved in the first 10 miles, the finish will be strong.

### General Tips

- Always run the tangent (shortest line through turns) — less distance traveled adds up over long races.
- Avoid headphones for the race experience (optional — personal preference).
- The true race starts in the final third of the distance, regardless of the race length.
